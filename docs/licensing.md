# Licence verification

For every actual media file, retain:

- Original publisher/creator source page and the date you inspected it.
- The exact licence grant/version and evidence it covers that file, not just a website or software repository.
- Whether redistribution, streaming, modification, and commercial hosting are permitted.
- Required attribution, modification notices, share-alike obligations, and source-code offers where applicable.
- Territory restrictions and public-domain status in the jurisdictions you serve.
- Separate rights for cover art, recordings, performances, translations, subtitles, fonts, and game assets.
- The actual file's format, byte size, checksum where relevant, and trusted source of acquisition.

“Free,” “abandonware,” and “available on the internet” are not redistribution licences. Freeware needs explicit permission. An open-source game's code licence does not automatically cover bundled art/music. A public-domain composition is not the same as a public-domain recording. A public-domain book may have a protected modern translation.

Display the source, licence link, and required attribution on the item page. Use an accurate badge (`Public Domain`, `CC0`, `CC BY`, `CC BY-SA`, `Open Source`, or `Freeware Redistribution Permitted`). Do not select a badge without source evidence. Publish nothing disputed until reviewed. Keep a real contact method on About and record how licence reports are handled.

The current music entry has incomplete source/licence metadata; the interface preserves its streaming URL but does not assert redistribution rights or offer downloads until those details are documented.
